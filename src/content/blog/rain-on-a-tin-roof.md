There is a particular sound that only exists in a Bangladeshi monsoon: heavy rain on a tin roof. It starts as a few loud drops, then turns into a steady roar that drowns out every other noise in the house.

When I was younger it meant one thing — no going outside. Now it feels like permission to stop. The world gets smaller, the light goes soft and grey, and there is nothing to do but sit with a cup of tea and listen.

## Why slow weather helps

Most days are full of small interruptions. Rain removes them. Plans get cancelled, roads empty out, and suddenly there is time to think properly about one thing instead of skimming across ten.

## A small habit

These days, when it starts to pour, I put my phone in another room and write down whatever is on my mind. No goal, no structure — just a page of thoughts while the roof keeps drumming.
